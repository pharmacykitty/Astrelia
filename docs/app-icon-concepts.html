Identity Study · iOS App

Astrolabe

App-icon directions & a final-name shortlist — drawn from the app itself: a point-at-the-sky planetarium with real astrophysics and a full astrology layer.

Every icon below is a hand-built SVG sharing the app's “luminous instrument” language — periwinkle light over deep space, serif-cool restraint, and the signature diffraction-spiked star the renderer already draws. Each is shown at icon size plus 80 px and 40 px so you can judge how it survives on the Home Screen and in Spotlight. Fifteen concepts, grouped by the idea they lead with.

Part One

The Name

The working title already fits unusually well: an astrolabe was the one instrument that did both jobs this app does — measure the real sky and cast a horoscope from it. That dual heritage is exactly our science-plus-symbolism split. Here's the case for keeping it, plus alternates if you want something more ownable.

Recommended · keep

Astrolabe

AS-troh-layb

The historical instrument for measuring star altitude, telling time, and — yes — casting charts. It literally means “star-taker.” Perfect thematic fit for an app that points at the sky and reads it astrologically. Risk: somewhat generic / possibly taken on the App Store. Mitigate with a distinct icon + a tagline lockup (“Astrolabe — chart the heavens”).

Alternate

Almagest

AL-muh-jest

Ptolemy's great star catalog — “The Greatest.” Carries both the astronomy and astrology lineage in one word, and is far more ownable than Astrolabe. Slightly academic; rewards the curious user the app is built for.

Alternate

Rete

REE-tee

The pierced, star-pointer disc that is the working part of an astrolabe — the open map of stars that rotates over the plate. Short, modern, ownable, and quietly insider. A great fit if you want a one-syllable mark.

Alternate

Caelum

KY-lum

Latin for “the heavens / chisel,” and also a real (if faint) constellation. Soft, elegant, gender-neutral; reads beautifully in a serif wordmark. Very ownable.

Bolder

Orrery

OR-uh-ree

A clockwork model of the heavens. Evokes the precision-instrument beauty you're chasing in the Galaxy Map and celestial sphere. Leans astronomy over astrology, but lovely and distinctive.

Bolder

Armilla

ar-MIL-uh

From the armillary sphere — rings of the heavens around the Earth. Maps directly onto the ★ celestial-sphere feature. Warmer and more name-like than “Armillary.”

Bolder

Empyrean

em-PEER-ee-an

The highest heaven, the realm of pure starlight. Romantic, premium, memorable. Less literal than the instrument names — sells the beauty pillar over the science one.

Wildcard

Sidereal

sy-DEER-ee-al

“Of the stars” — and the literal name of the star-time and zodiac system the engine computes. A technical word the right audience will love; harder to spell on first hearing.

My pick: keep Astrolabe if the App Store name is free — nothing else fits the dual mission as cleanly. If it's taken or you want something more uniquely yours, Almagest and Rete are the strongest fallbacks, in that order.

Part Two

The Icon

Five families: the namesake instruments, the signature star, the celestial sphere, the act of looking, and the cosmos itself. The strongest at thumbnail size are #2, #5, #3, #13 and #14 — noted on each card.

1The Rete

Family · Namesake instrument

Inspiration
A literal astrolabe seen face-on: graduated brass limb, the off-centre eccentric circle of the ecliptic, the rule, and the pierced star-pointer “flames” of the rete, each ending in a real star.
Meaning
The most on-the-nose telling of the name — this is an astrolabe. It says “precision instrument for the heavens” before you open the app, and bridges science + symbolism the way the device historically did.
Details
Brass-gold over deep space for an heirloom, hand-tool feel. Richest of the set — gorgeous large, but watch legibility at 40 px; the simplest reading is the limb + central pin.
also at

2The Diffraction Star ★

Family · Signature star · top thumbnail

Inspiration
The exact star the app already renders — a four-point diffraction-spiked sparkle with a glowing core and a soft atmosphere halo, the way the catalog and celestial sphere draw the brightest stars.
Meaning
Pure, confident, unmistakable. One real star, beautifully lit — the whole app distilled to its atom. It promises light and wonder, not features.
Details
Survives shrinking better than anything here and is instantly “Astrolabe” once the brand is known. White-to-periwinkle core; companion sparkles keep it from feeling like a generic asterisk. The safest, most scalable choice.
also at

3The Celestial Sphere ★

Family · Celestial sphere · the moat feature

Inspiration
The ★ differentiator screen — the rotating luminous globe — reduced to three great circles through one sphere: the cyan celestial equator, the gold ecliptic tilted at 23.5°, and the periwinkle meridian, with an atmosphere rim.
Meaning
This is the icon that says “we actually have a sky engine.” The crossing circles are the coordinate transforms that are the spine of the whole project; the gold ecliptic is where astronomy hands off to the zodiac.
Details
Three colours encode the three frames (horizon / equator / ecliptic). Reads as a globe even small; the tilted gold ring is the memorable silhouette. The most “this app and no other” concept.
also at

4The Reticle

Family · The act of looking

Inspiration
The AR “point at the sky” core interaction — the centre reticle and cardinal-tick HUD from Sky mode, framing a bright star you've just aimed at and identified.
Meaning
Leads with the verb: this is the app you raise to the heavens to take a sighting. Active, exploratory, a little sci-fi cockpit.
Details
The dashed graduated ring + crosshair gap read as an instrument, not a camera app. Risk of looking like a generic AR/target icon — the star in the centre is what keeps it ours.
also at

5The Ecliptic Ring ★

Family · Celestial sphere · top thumbnail

Inspiration
A single tilted ring — the ecliptic / zodiac belt — banking in perspective around a glowing central star, with one planet bead riding the band. Saturn-ring elegance meets the wheel of the zodiac.
Meaning
The cleanest fusion of the two layers: the star (astrophysics) inside the ring of the path of the Sun (astrology). Calm, premium, and very ownable as a silhouette.
Details
The strong 3D bank gives instant depth and brand-shape recognition. Shown here in violet, but recolours cleanly to periwinkle or gold. Among the best small-size performers.
also at

6The Asterism “A”

Family · The cosmos · wordmark tie-in

Inspiration
The constellation browser's connect-the-dots figures, bent into a stylised letter “A” — five catalog stars joined by faint sightlines.
Meaning
A monogram that's also a constellation: the brand initial written in stars. Playful, friendly, immediately “app-like,” and a natural for a wordmark lockup.
Details
Most legible monogram here; the trade-off is it leans cute over awe and is the least uniquely-astronomical. Great as a secondary / marketing mark even if not the primary.

7The Armillary

Family · Namesake instrument

Inspiration
An armillary sphere — graduated brass rings of the heavens (equator, meridian, ecliptic band) around a polar axis, with a star at the heart.
Meaning
The “antique cosmology made beautiful” pillar. Companion to the celestial-sphere concept but rendered as a physical, hand-made object rather than a glowing globe.
Details
Warm brass = heritage and craft. Detailed, so best appreciated large; at 40 px it simplifies to a ringed orb. Pairs naturally with the “Armilla” name.
also at

8The Galaxy

Family · The cosmos

Inspiration
Galaxy Map mode — the stylised barred spiral with a blazing core and the Sun marked as a single sparkle out on one arm.
Meaning
Sells the scale and the Elite-Dangerous free-flight fantasy: leave Earth and fly the real local star field. The marked Sun is the “you are here” promise.
Details
Violet + cyan arms over deep space — the most overtly “space app” of the set, with the upside and the cliché risk that implies. The dual-colour arms keep it from being a stock swirl.
also at

9The Crescent

Family · The cosmos · astrology tint

Inspiration
The app's astrology accent — moon.stars.fill in warm gold — a crescent cradling the signature star.
Meaning
Speaks directly to the astrology / charts audience and the “Tonight” moon-phase feed. Warm, inviting, lifestyle-friendly.
Details
Honest about a trade-off: this is the most conventional celestial icon and risks reading as “just another astrology app,” which undersells the science half. Included as the gold-family option and a possible alternate app icon.

10The Horizon Dome

Family · The act of looking

Inspiration
The alt-az projection itself: a graduated horizon with the north marker, vertical meridian and altitude arcs sweeping up to a zenith star — the geometry Sky mode draws every frame.
Meaning
“You, standing under the real sky, here and now.” The most grounded, observational concept — it foregrounds your horizon, not abstract space.
Details
Distinctive composition (anchored to a baseline rather than centred). Loses its fine ticks when tiny, simplifying to dome-plus-star, which still reads.

11The Sextant

Family · Namesake instrument

Inspiration
A navigator's sextant — graduated arc, frame, and an index arm swung up to “shoot” a star. A cousin of the astrolabe: measuring altitude to find your place.
Meaning
Wayfinding and precision. Ties the act of sighting a star to literally locating yourself — the sensor-fusion heart of the app.
Details
Asymmetric, dynamic composition with strong diagonal energy. Cyan instrument over deep blue. Reads as “tool,” which differentiates it from every soft celestial-blob competitor.

12The System

Family · The cosmos

Inspiration
The exoplanet “fly to a star, see its planets” view — a host star with concentric, tilted orbits, coloured planet beads, and a faint green habitable-zone ring.
Meaning
Depth and discovery — the reward for curiosity. Says “there are real worlds in here,” which is the exoplanet layer's whole promise.
Details
The off-axis ellipses give 3D life. Risk: orbit motifs can read “science textbook / atom.” The diffraction star at the centre (not a dot) keeps it celestial rather than chemical.

13The Lodestar Rose ★

Family · The act of looking · top thumbnail

Inspiration
A compass / wind rose fused with a star — the long north ray reaching up to a pole star, on a graduated bezel. Navigation meets the sky.
Meaning
Orientation and true north — the app references everything to true north via the sensors. A timeless “find your way by the stars” symbol with built-in gravitas.
Details
Bold, symmetric, crest-like silhouette that reads beautifully tiny — one of the best thumbnail performers. Familiar enough to trust, distinctive in the star treatment.
also at

14The Aperture ★

Family · Signature star · top thumbnail

Inspiration
The most reduced idea: three concentric hairline rings — the astrolabe's plates, an aperture, an iris on the cosmos — closing onto a single luminous star. The “luminous instrument” design system itself, as a mark.
Meaning
Focus, depth, looking in to look out. Pure abstraction means maximum ownability and zero cliché — it could only be this app.
Details
The most minimal, modern, and timeless option; scales perfectly and ages well. A graduated dashed outer ring is the only nod to the instrument heritage. Quiet — its strength and its risk.
also at

15The Chart Wheel

Family · The cosmos · astrology

Inspiration
The ChartWheel — a twelve-spoke zodiac ring with aspect chords crossing the interior, drawn in gold.
Meaning
The astrology module made literal: houses, signs, and the geometry of aspects. Speaks fluently to chart-readers.
Details
Like #9, this is the explicitly-astrology option and risks under-selling the science half. Strongest as an alternate app icon users could switch to, or the Astrology tab's mark — pairing well with #3 or #5 as the primary.
